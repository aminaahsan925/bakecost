Try BakeCost with sample invoices

This is how BakeCost works. Three sample supplier invoices, a price history built from them, and three sample recipes recosted at the latest prices. Change the margin target, add your own invoice line, and export the table. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

About the sample numbers. The dollar prices are illustrative. The end to end moves for eggs (down 35%), butter (down 17%) and wheat flour (up 24%) match the public figures on the home page: US Bureau of Labor Statistics prices, September 2025 to September 2026, and French wheat flour, 12 months to October 2026. The middle invoice and the sugar prices are illustrative.

1. Sample supplier invoices

Three invoices from one sample supplier, across a year. Each one lists what every ingredient cost on that date.

2. Price history per ingredient

One price line per ingredient, built from the invoices above. This is the table a baker would otherwise keep by hand.

3. Recipes recosted at the latest prices

Cost then is the September 2025 price set. Cost now is the latest price set. Margin is at today’s selling price, ingredient cost only (packaging, labour and overheads not included).

4. Add your own invoice line

Add a price you paid, and watch the price history and the recipe margins update. This stays in your browser only.

5. Export the costing table

Download the current recipe costing table as a CSV file, ready to open in a spreadsheet.
